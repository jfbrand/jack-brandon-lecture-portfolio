Task A: Portfolio Analysis

Portfolio 1: https://sites.google.com/view/sethschafferportfolio/home

Portfolio example one is from Seth Schaffer who's main focus has been primarily in the construction of combat robots. Immediately, the reader's eyes are drawn to the many photos and video attachments on the home page that display the projects and products that he has worked on. This lends the reader to be able to find and learn more about each project or potentially purchase one of his products in less than a minute. His portfolio content is structured in a way that is oriented more toward advertisement for his YouTube channel and product site so naturally he does not focus on documenting the details of the design or construction of said products, thus negatively effecting the reproducibility. However, the content within his "combat robots" tab is slightly more detailed and provides the reader with many photos of the CAD models and construction of his robots. He does briefly highlight the design intent and inner mechanics for his later robot designs and links to some videos from his channel that document some of the CNC machining process he utilized in his latest project. He also gave some insight into his iterative process beginning with his first design and its weaknesses/failure modes and how he addressed these in his second. For example, the issue of the first robot not being able to flip itself over was addressed in the arm design of the second robot. Overall, the language in Seth's portfolio is more casual than professional and would likely not be presentable to an employer due to the conversational tone as opposed to a more structured "problem-solution" format. However, it is likely that professionalism was not his intent as this was explained to be more of a "side hustle" for him and is evident in the hobbyist and entrepreneurial tone of his portfolio.

Portfolio 2: https://nhoong.github.io/

Portfolio example two is from Nathan Hoong. Nathan's portfolio highlights four of his mechanical design projects ranging from surgical tools and medical product test equipment to automotive components/assemblies. The layout of his portfolio lends itself to clarity and a sub-one-minute navigation time for potential employers and fellow engineers alike. Each product visual outlines his objective and result providing design intent and implementation. He provides CAD assembly pictures for each design, respectively and for his "shock top" design, an exploded view, individual components and a BOM. His level of detail is likely satisfactory for an employer or business looking to learn about his designs, but an engineer would benefit from seeing more consistent documentation across his portfolio. Also, the design process is not described at all for any of Nathan's projects. He would benefit from highlighting how and why he made the design decisions he did, instead of just explaining the final result for each. Despite these deficiencies, the tone of the portfolio is professional in both the structure and tone. His stark layout embodies a corporate style that would likely be attractive to prospective employers and his language is similarly focused. The tone of his portfolio aligns with his other decisions to focus on less of the engineering and more of the marketing of his accomplishments to corporate hiring teams and management.
